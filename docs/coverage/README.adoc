= Reference-to-corpus inventory

This document records, by authority, the reference documents in the
Interscript project's reference collection, which is the archive of
standards documents against which the maps are built and verified, and
the maps in the corpus that implement them. A reference document is listed whether or not the
corpus provides a map for it, and each map is named with the reference
standard from which it was encoded when that standard is in the
collection. The corpus is the authority for what is encoded.

== ICS 630-01

ICS 630-01 is the Intelligence Community standard for foreign-language
transliteration. The standard establishes methods for rendering
personal names written in non-Roman scripts into the Roman alphabet and
delegates the language-specific tables to annexes administered by the
Senior Language Authority of each IC element. The project's reference
collection contains 29 documents for the standard: the standard itself,
23 language annexes lettered A to W, three Chinese appendices, and
revisions of Annexes J and M.

Twenty-one of the 23 language annexes have maps in the corpus. The
Chinese annex (D) and the Kurdish annex (G) do not.

=== Chinese annex

Annex D applies to personal names already rendered in Hanyu Pinyin and
standardises that rendering: surname capitalization, omission of the
umlaut on u, spacing, and apostrophes. The annex assumes the pinyin
form and does not supply a lexicon for the conversion of Han characters
to pinyin. Its appendices are Appendix A, the IC transliteration table;
Appendix B, conversion tables from Wade-Giles, Yale, and Mandarin
Phonetic to pinyin; and Appendix C, a list of common Chinese surnames. A
pinyin normalisation map and Latn-to-Latn conversion maps can be
encoded from the annex. A Han-to-pinyin map requires a character
lexicon, and the corpus provides `bgnpcgn-zho-Hans-Latn-1979` for that
conversion.

=== Kurdish annex

Annex G is a Perso-Arabic (Sorani) to Latin table for personal names.
The table uses the 26 Latin letters and the apostrophe, without
diacritics. The corpus's `bgnpcgn-kur-Arab-Latn-2007` implements a
different authority and does not substitute for the annex.

== ODNI reference folder

The folder contains documents for Arabic, Belarusian, Bulgarian,
Farsi/Persian and Dari, Hindi/Urdu, Korean, Kurdish, Macedonian,
Pashto, Serbian, and Ukrainian. The corpus provides ODNI maps for ten of
the eleven languages. The Kurdish document is the same table as Annex G
and has no map in the corpus. The Chinese annex is in the ICS 630-01
collection.

== BGN/PCGN

The BGN/PCGN systems represented in the corpus and the reference tables
that have no map are listed in `bgnpcgn-status.adoc` in this directory.

=== Historical editions

The collection contains 16 documents in the 1964 BGN romanization guide
and 30 documents in the 1994 USBGN romanization guide. These documents
record earlier editions of systems whose current BGN/PCGN sheets are
also in the collection, including Hebrew (1962), Burmese (1970), Khmer
(1972), and Lao (1966).

== ALA-LC

The official catalogue of 78 ALA-LC romanization tables is captured in
`alalc-roman.html`, and differences against the corpus are recorded in
`alalc-diff.txt`. The collection contains one table document,
`alalc/1997/mongolia.pdf`. The corpus contains 55 ALA-LC maps, whose
source tables are not in the collection. Encoding the 47 catalog
systems that are not in the corpus requires acquisition of their table
documents. Several of those systems overlap languages represented under
other authorities, including Burmese, Khmer, Lao, Shan, Hebrew, Pashto,
Thai, Urdu, Uyghur, Uzbek, and Kurdish.

== ICAO

The collection contains ICAO 9303 Part 3 (Amendment 1) and the 9303
Arabic helper. The corpus contains eight ICAO maps. The Part 3 in the
collection does not contain the language transliteration tables, which
are published in another part or edition of 9303.

== BAS, UN, and other authorities

* The two BAS documents have maps: `bas-rus-Cyrl-Latn-2017-bss` and
  `bas-rus-Cyrl-Latn-2017-oss`.
* The corpus contains 32 UN maps, and the UNGEGN schedules are not in
  the collection.
* The remaining single-system authorities, including ELOT, MVD, GOST,
  MEXT, MOCT, LSHK, ACADSIN, SAC, HK, and KP, have verified maps in the
  corpus and no source document in the collection.

== Reference tables not yet encoded

Twenty-five reference tables in the collection have no map in the
corpus: two ICS annexes, five DIN standards, two BSI standards, and
sixteen BGN/PCGN systems. The BGN/PCGN systems are listed in
`bgnpcgn-status.adoc`; the others are listed in their authority
sections above.

== Neural model coverage

The deterministic map and the neural model are independent products.
The corpus can provide a map for a language without a published model,
and most languages in the corpus have no model. Neural models are
published for Hebrew, Khmer, Thai, Persian, Arabic, and Urdu. No neural
model is published for Burmese, Dzongkha, Inuktitut, Lao, or Shan,
because no parallel corpus of source-script names with romanized
equivalents is available for training in those languages.
