= Reference-to-corpus inventory

This document records, by authority, the reference documents in the
Interscript project's reference collection, which is the archive of
standards documents against which the maps are built and verified, and
the maps in the corpus that implement them. A reference document is listed whether or not the
corpus provides a map for it, and each map is named with the reference
standard from which it was encoded when that standard is in the
collection. The corpus is the authority for what is encoded.

== ICS 630-01

ICS 630-01 is the Intelligence Community standard for foreign-language
transliteration. The standard establishes methods for rendering
personal names written in non-Roman scripts into the Roman alphabet and
delegates the language-specific tables to annexes administered by the
Senior Language Authority of each IC element. The project's reference
collection contains 29 documents for the standard: the standard itself,
23 language annexes lettered A to W, three Chinese appendices, and
revisions of Annexes J and M.

Twenty-one of the 23 language annexes have maps in the corpus. The
Chinese annex (D) and the Kurdish annex (G) do not.

=== Chinese annex

Annex D applies to personal names already rendered in Hanyu Pinyin and
standardises that rendering: surname capitalization, omission of the
umlaut on u, spacing, and apostrophes. The annex assumes the pinyin
form and does not supply a lexicon for the conversion of Han characters
to pinyin. Its appendices are Appendix A, the IC transliteration table;
Appendix B, conversion tables from Wade-Giles, Yale, and Mandarin
Phonetic to pinyin; and Appendix C, a list of common Chinese surnames. A
pinyin normalisation map and Latn-to-Latn conversion maps can be
encoded from the annex. A Han-to-pinyin map requires a character
lexicon, and the corpus provides `bgnpcgn-zho-Hans-Latn-1979` for that
conversion.

=== Kurdish annex

Annex G is a Perso-Arabic (Sorani) to Latin table for personal names.
The table uses the 26 Latin letters and the apostrophe, without
diacritics. The corpus's `bgnpcgn-kur-Arab-Latn-2007` implements a
different authority and does not substitute for the annex.

== ODNI reference folder

The folder contains documents for Arabic, Belarusian, Bulgarian,
Farsi/Persian and Dari, Hindi/Urdu, Korean, Kurdish, Macedonian,
Pashto, Serbian, and Ukrainian. The corpus provides ODNI maps for ten of
the eleven languages. The Kurdish document is the same table as Annex G
and has no map in the corpus. The Chinese annex is in the ICS 630-01
collection.

=== IS 13194:1991 decisions

The Annex F scheme renders Tamil/Malayalam zha and Devanagari
lla-nukta with a dotted z; the maps use the dotted-z forms consistently.
Gurmukhi letters with no Annex A correspondence (the standalone vowel
carriers) keep the corpus's prior readings, flagged in the maps rather
than invented against the source. Amendment No. 1 (December 2010)
assigns ISCII position FC to the Indian Rupee symbol; the symbol is
already Latin and passes through unchanged - recorded in every map's
notes (the amendment postdates the 1991 scope).
All three ISO documents in the collection have maps in the corpus. The
corpus's other ISO maps, which implement ISO 233, ISO 843, ISO 3602,
ISO 11940, ISO 15919, ISO 259, and ISO 259-2, have no source document in
the collection.

=== ISO 11940:1998 interim source

The corpus's `iso-tha-Thai-Latn-11940-1998` was verified and completed
against the Wikipedia presentation of ISO 11940
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_11940, CC BY-SA 4.0, retrieved
2026-11-19) as the interim working source, per the full-scope
transcription rule: every table of that presentation (the consonant
presentation table, the vowel-sign table, the other-combining-marks
table, and punctuation and digits) is embedded as per-row tests from
the provenance artifact
`scripts/row-sources/iso-tha-Thai-Latn-11940-1998.json`, alongside the
presentation's two worked examples. One divergence was fixed: phinthu
U+0E3A emitted a spurious en dash before ring below U+0325. The
standard's paiyannoi/angkhandiao distinction (ǂ vs ǀ) is
unrepresentable from Unicode, which encodes both uses as U+0E2F; the
map carries the paiyannoi reading and records the limitation in its
notes, together with the ICU/CLDR divergences that were checked and
deliberately not reproduced (prime for horn, ả for ำ, ˌ for phinthu,
transposed preposed vowels). U+0E3F (baht) is outside the
working source's tables and passes through unchanged. ISO 11940 moves
from purchase-needed to encoded-with-interim-source; the official ISO
11940:1998 document remains the purchase of record for provenance (the
owner's P2 acquisition list).

The held copy of SIST ISO 843:2005 is an identical-text adoption of
ISO 843:1997 and is recorded as informative only; the verification
against it adds nothing beyond the official ISO 843:1997 edition.

=== ISO/R 843:1968 held-copy gaps

Two of the recommendation's example lines (clause 1(b) rough-breathing
demonstration, and the uppercase Myrtoon line of clause 1(d)) are
partially obscured by the watermark of the held scan; their readings
are flagged in the map notes and are not pinned as tests. The printed
lowercase Myrtoion output shows u/y glyphs ambiguous at scan resolution;
row 20 (Y, y -> U, u) governs. The footnote separator dot (tau-chi,
tau-sigma) is rendered by the map as a full stop because the printed
mid dot collides with the ano-teleia -> colon rule.

=== ISO 15919, both editions

ISO 15919 transliterates Devanagari and related Indic scripts into
Latin. The corpus implements both editions:

* First edition (2001): 19 language-scoped maps for the ten main
  scripts (Devanagari, Bengali, Assamese, Gurmukhi, Gujarati, Odia,
  Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam) and the Pali/Prakrit/Sanskrit/
  Hindi/Marathi/Nepali language maps, plus the 2026-11 remainder round:
  the generic Sinhala map `iso-sin-Sinh-Latn-15919-2001` (the vocalic
  vowel rows follow the ISO r-ring-below family per the normative
  sources, with the corpus's Pali-scoped divergences recorded as
  findings, not fixes), the Kaithi map `iso-inc-Kthi-Latn-15919-2001`,
  and the per-script extension cells Malayalam U+0D3A → ṯa and
  Kannada U+0CDE → ḻa added to `iso-mal-Mlym-Latn-15919-2001` and
  `iso-kan-Kana-Latn-15919-2001`. The first edition's Vedic accents
  (udatta acute, independent svarita grave, anudatta underscore over
  the transliterated vowel) are output-accent rules rather than static
  substitutions; they are documented in the notes of the corpus's
  2001 Devanagari maps. The Perso-Arabic nuqta column of the first
  edition is encoded in `iso-san-Deva-Latn-15919-2001`.
* Second edition (DIS stage, ISO/TC 46/WG 3 N125): 17 script tables
  7.1-7.17 encoded as `iso-*-15919-2026` maps (Devanagari, Bengali and
  Assamese, Gurmukhi, Gujarati, Odia, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada,
  Malayalam, Sinhala, Brahmi, Grantha, Kaithi, Modi, Sharada, Takri,
  Newa), including the clause-6 virama list, Rule 17 Vedic accents
  (U+1CF5 → U+1E96, U+1CF6 → U+1E2B, encoded in the Devanagari map),
  the Annex A numerals, and options 9.8/9.9/9.10 as selectable
  variants (`languages opt98 opt99 opt10`). Lao, Khmer, Thai, and
  Tibetan do not appear anywhere in the DIS text (verified by
  full-document scan against audit #300); Annex B scripts are
  informative and clause 11 places their mappings outside the scope of
  the standard.

The second edition is encoded from the committee ballot draft and is
not yet a published International Standard; each map's notes record
the ballot document and the DIS errata found during encoding.

The published first edition (ISO 15919:2001) is in the project's
reference collection and adjudicates the 2001-edition maps: the
Sinhala vocalic vowels are the ring-below family (Table 10 refs
141-144); the Vedic accent letters jihvamuliya and upadhmaniya are
transliterated h with dot below + combining macron below and h with
dot below + combining breve below (Table 1 refs 003a/003b, Table 8
refs 131a/131b), not the second edition's single-character forms; the
Devanagari eyelash-ra row (Table 1 ref 048a) prints r with breve; the
official Devanagari table carries the Perso-Arabic nuqta column
(refs 088-094, encoded); the official Kannada table prints U+0CDE as
la with line below (ref 222) and the za/fa values as nukta
combinations; the official Malayalam table prints no ttta row (the
U+0D3A row of modern secondary transcriptions is out of scope); the
official Telugu table prints ardhasunna as n with breve (ref 001,
Rule 7), candrabindu as m with dot below (ref 001a) and la with line
below (ref 052a); the official Tamil table prints no anusvara row.
Rows of the official tables that use characters outside ISO/IEC
10646-1:2000 which Unicode never adopted, and the Sinhala table's
Pali-usage alternative readings, are documented in the maps' notes.

ISO/TR 11941:1996 source note: the corpus's two method maps are encoded
from the owner-held complete document (`iso/ISO-TR-11941-1996.pdf`,
11 pp., including section 4 transliteration tables with their notes and
sections 5-7). The Slovenian adoption file SIST-TP ISO/TR 11941:2005
held outside the collection is an iTeh STANDARD PREVIEW containing only
sections 1-3 (the scope and the general conversion principles, printed
pages 1-3 of 6) and none of the transliteration tables; it cannot
support encoding and must not be used as a substitute for the complete
document.

== BGN/PCGN

The BGN/PCGN systems represented in the corpus and the reference tables
that have no map are listed in `bgnpcgn-status.adoc` in this directory.

=== Historical editions

The collection contains 16 documents in the 1964 BGN romanization guide
and 30 documents in the 1994 USBGN romanization guide. These documents
record earlier editions of systems whose current BGN/PCGN sheets are
also in the collection, including Hebrew (1962), Burmese (1970), Khmer
(1972), and Lao (1966).

== ALA-LC

The official catalogue of 78 ALA-LC romanization tables is captured in
`alalc-roman.html`, and differences against the corpus are recorded in
`alalc-diff.txt`. The collection contains one table document,
`alalc/1997/mongolia.pdf`. The corpus contains 55 ALA-LC maps, whose
source tables are not in the collection. Encoding the 47 catalog
systems that are not in the corpus requires acquisition of their table
documents. Several of those systems overlap languages represented under
other authorities, including Burmese, Khmer, Lao, Shan, Hebrew, Pashto,
Thai, Urdu, Uyghur, Uzbek, and Kurdish.

== ICAO

The collection contains ICAO 9303 Part 3 (Amendment 1) and the 9303
Arabic helper. The corpus contains eight ICAO maps. The Part 3 in the
collection does not contain the language transliteration tables, which
are published in another part or edition of 9303.

== BAS, UN, and other authorities

* The two BAS documents have maps: `bas-rus-Cyrl-Latn-2017-bss` and
  `bas-rus-Cyrl-Latn-2017-oss`.
* The corpus contains 32 UN maps, and the UNGEGN schedules are not in
  the collection.
* The remaining single-system authorities, including ELOT, MVD, GOST,
  MEXT, MOCT, LSHK, ACADSIN, SAC, HK, and KP, have verified maps in the
  corpus and no source document in the collection.

== Academic sources

The collection's `academic/` folder holds scholarly systems outside the
standards bodies. The first such document, the University of Tokyo
Komaba "Recommended System for Romanizing Japanese" (2009), backs the
new `var-jpn-Hrkt-Latn-komaba-2009`, encoded per the var-* scholarly
family conventions. Its morpheme-dependent provisions (vowel-sequence
repeats, word division, honorific hyphenation) are recorded as
limitations in the map notes.

== Reference tables not yet encoded

Twenty-five reference tables in the collection have no map in the
corpus: two ICS annexes, five DIN standards, two BSI standards, and
sixteen BGN/PCGN systems. The BGN/PCGN systems are listed in
`bgnpcgn-status.adoc`; the others are listed in their authority
sections above.

== Neural model coverage

The deterministic map and the neural model are independent products.
The corpus can provide a map for a language without a published model,
and most languages in the corpus have no model. Neural models are
published for Hebrew, Khmer, Thai, Persian, Arabic, and Urdu. No neural
model is published for Burmese, Dzongkha, Inuktitut, Lao, or Shan,
because no parallel corpus of source-script names with romanized
equivalents is available for training in those languages.
